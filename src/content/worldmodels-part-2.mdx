# WorldModels Part 2: The Vision Model

In Part 1, we established the environment and generated $10,000$ perturbed trajectories to ensure comprehensive out-of-distribution (OOD) coverage. The next architectural challenge is dimensionality reduction.

Operating a Reinforcement Learning policy directly on raw pixel arrays ($64 \times 64 \times 3 = 12,288$ dimensions) is computationally inefficient and highly susceptible to noise. The goal of the Vision Model is to compress this high-dimensional observation space into a low-dimensional, continuous latent vector $z \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$.

## 1. Spatial Awareness via CoordConv

A standard Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) is inherently translation-invariant. While this is highly beneficial for image classification, our control task relies exclusively on precise spatial localization—the agent must know _exactly_ where the car is on the track.

To break the translation invariance and give the filters access to absolute spatial coordinates, the encoder utilizes a **CoordConv** layer. We generate two normalized coordinate grids $C_x$ and $C_y$ ranging from $-1.0$ to $1.0$:

$$ C_x^{(i,j)} = \frac{2j}{W-1} - 1, \quad C_y^{(i,j)} = \frac{2i}{H-1} - 1 $$

These coordinate grids are concatenated to the original RGB input tensor $X$, transforming the 3-channel image into a 5-channel tensor before the first convolution:

$$ X\_{\text{coord}} = \text{Concat}(X, C_x, C_y) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 5 \times H \times W} $$

By explicitly providing $(X, Y)$ coordinates to the filters, the encoder easily learns a continuous mapping from the car's visual position to a precise latent representation.

## 2. The Custom Masked Loss Function

Training a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) on an environment where 99% of the pixels are static background presents a significant challenge: Mode collapse. If we use a standard Mean Squared Error (MSE), the network quickly learns that completely ignoring the tiny $4 \times 2$ pixel car and outputting a static gray track yields a near-perfect loss.

To force the network to allocate latent capacity to the car, I designed a **Masked Binary Cross Entropy (BCE)** loss.

Since the car is red and the track is gray ($R=G=B$), we can dynamically generate a boolean mask during training by measuring the absolute difference between the blue and red channels:

$$ M = \begin{cases} 10 & \text{if } |X*{\text{blue}} - X*{\text{red}}| > \delta \\ 1 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$

We then apply this multiplier matrix to the standard BCE loss, scaling the penalty for car-pixels by a factor of 10. The total loss function combines this masked reconstruction loss with the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, regulated by a $\beta$-parameter to prevent the latent space from collapsing:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{Total}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum \left( M \odot \text{BCE}(X, \hat{X}) \right) + \beta \cdot D_{\text{KL}}(q(z|x) \,||\, \mathcal{N}(0, I)) $$

## 3. Training Dynamics and the Grokking Phenomenon

By tracking the reconstructed images and loss metrics over the training epochs, we can observe a fascinating optimization behavior often referred to as delayed generalization or **Grokking**.

Use the slider below to scrub through the optimization process epoch by epoch:

<TrainingEvolution />

Observe the specific dynamics up to **Epoch 7**. The network falls into a deceptive local minimum: it aggressively minimizes the KL Divergence (orange line) while the reconstruction and validation losses quickly hit a plateau. Because the model hasn't yet learned to encode spatial information, it takes the statistical "lazy route", smearing the car across all possible positions. The result is a highly blurred, homogeneous **red ring**.

Right around **Epoch 8**, a phase transition occurs. The network finally discovers how to leverage the `CoordConv` channels to map the latent vectors to a discrete Cartesian position.

Notice the detail in the metrics at this exact moment: **The KL Loss suddenly spikes**, breaking its downward trend. To encode the precise position of the car, the encoder is forced to push real information through the bottleneck, deviating from the perfect Gaussian prior.

Simultaneously, the red ring condenses into a localized red blob, causing the reconstruction and validation losses to break out of their plateau and drop sharply (with the overall training loss and reconstruction loss sitting almost perfectly on top of each other, as reconstruction dominates the penalty). Over the remaining epochs, the track turns back to solid gray and the car becomes perfectly localized.
